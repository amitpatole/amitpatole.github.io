Verified AI, built in the open.

A family of tools for AI systems where nothing is “done” until a grader returns a verdict — the Verel organism and a set of standalone products. Each links to its own documentation.

Amit began his career as one of the first engineers at a small firm in Pune, where he sourced the hardware, designed the network, and built the production infrastructure from the ground up. That experience of building from nothing became a habit of mind: understand the foundation before touching the surface, and never call something done until you can prove it holds.

The two decades that followed ran through enterprise build-and-release engineering, multi-vendor deployments on carrier-grade private cloud, and Kubernetes platforms serving hundreds of microservices across global datacenters. At Viavi Solutions he became the primary architect for telecom platforms serving major operators across multiple continents, shipping AI tools now sold as the Viavi AI Assistant and anchoring them to real production data from Tier-1 operators. He led teams across time zones, served as the designated crisis-escalation lead for production, and drove the kind of infrastructure automation that makes the difference between a team that ships and one that maintains.

The AI work sharpened a question he had been circling for years: when a model produces an output, what does it mean for the result to be genuinely finished? Not that a pipeline succeeded or a test passed. Done in the sense that an engineer would stake their name on it. A 2026 paper explored one edge of that question, asking whether quantum-optimization methods can find cost-optimal routing across a multi-agent LLM cascade where classical approaches fall short.
